Water Quality Around the Globe

Tap water safety varies dramatically depending on municipal filtration systems, mineral content, source water quality, and the age of piping infrastructure. In countries like Switzerland, Singapore, and Finland, tap water is cleaner and better regulated than bottled water. In many other places, drinking tap water can cause serious illness even for locals.

Boil Notices and Travel Tips

In countries where tap water is not safe, stick to sealed bottled water, filtered bottles, or boiled water â€” even for brushing your teeth. Avoid ice cubes in drinks unless you know they were made with purified water. Street food is often fine when cooked, but raw vegetables washed in tap water can be a hidden source of contamination.

Why Water Quality Varies

Infrastructure investment, geographic water sources, climate, and regulatory standards all play a role. Developed countries with cold climates and modern treatment plants tend to have the safest tap water. Developing countries with older infrastructure, warmer climates, and less regulation face higher contamination risks from bacteria, heavy metals, and agricultural runoff.

Staying Healthy While Traveling

Your body is not adapted to local bacteria in a new place, so even water that is technically safe for locals can cause digestive issues for visitors. Start with bottled or filtered water and give your system time to adjust. A reusable filtered water bottle is one of the best investments you can make for travel health and reducing plastic waste.
