Navigating International Etiquette

Gestures, tipping habits, dress codes, and body language vary so widely across borders that what is perfectly polite in one country can be deeply offensive in another. Most travelers do not mean to cause offense — they just do not know what they do not know.

Top Taboos Worldwide

Showing the soles of your feet is insulting in Thailand and many Arab countries. Tipping in Japan can be seen as rude because good service is expected by default. In France, greeting without a polite "bonjour" first is considered disrespectful regardless of what follows.

Why It Matters

Getting local etiquette right changes how locals perceive you. A traveler who makes an effort with local customs is welcomed warmly, while one who ignores them may be tolerated but never trusted. In business contexts, these nuances can make or break deals and partnerships.

How to Use This Tool

Select your destination before you travel and get a customized list of do's and don'ts covering greetings, dining, tipping, dress, gift-giving, and conversation topics. Save or screenshot it for the plane — it takes five minutes to read and could save you from some deeply awkward moments.
