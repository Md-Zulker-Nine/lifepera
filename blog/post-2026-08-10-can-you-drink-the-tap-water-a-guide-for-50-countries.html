How to Check Tap-Water Safety Before You Travel

LifePera·August 10, 2026

There is no dependable single yes-or-no answer for an entire country. Drinking-water conditions can vary by municipality, building, supply system, and current advisory. This article does not publish a country safety ranking: the earlier country-by-country classifications were not backed by a documented dataset and should not be relied on.

Check the exact place and current advice

Before drinking tap water, check guidance for your destination and the specific town or accommodation. Ask your host or hotel whether the building has a current advisory or private storage tank, and follow notices from the local water utility or public-health authority. A broad country label cannot confirm the condition of an individual tap today.

The U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) provides food and water safety guidance for travelers, including precautions when water quality is uncertain. Use destination-specific official advice where available, and check again close to travel because notices can change.

If the water source is uncertain

Follow local public-health instructions. CDC guidance describes options such as factory-sealed drinks and treating water, but the right method depends on the suspected hazard: boiling or disinfection does not remove every chemical contaminant, and a filter's performance depends on its specifications and correct use. Do not assume a generic travel filter makes any source safe.

For detailed steps and limitations, read the CDC's water disinfection guidance. Follow product instructions and local advisories rather than relying on a country list, taste, or appearance to judge safety.

Consider food, ice, and oral care too

CDC travel guidance notes that water can be involved in cooking, washing food, preparing drinks, making ice, and brushing teeth. If local advice says the tap water may be contaminated, use the recommended safe water source for these activities as well. Ask about ice and drinks if you cannot confirm how they were prepared.

When to seek individual advice

General travel guidance is not a personal medical assessment. If you have a condition that increases your risk from infection, are traveling with an infant, or need advice about a specific destination, ask a qualified clinician or travel-health service before departure. If you become seriously ill during travel, seek local medical care.

Sources and scope

This page offers a way to check current information; it does not assess the safety of any country's water supply, hotel, building, or tap. The CDC resources above provide general travel precautions, not a real-time test for your location. Always follow current local public-health and utility instructions.
